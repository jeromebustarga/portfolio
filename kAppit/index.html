The pins are real coordinates: 26 Filipino businesses, 10 events and 12 people across National City and Chula Vista.

In Chula Vista, about one in eight people is Filipino. You can still go months without meeting a single one.

Filipino community abroad moves through church groups, family networks and word of mouth. Every one of those needs you to already know somebody. If you have just arrived — a student, a nurse on a new contract, a family that moved for work — you are outside all of them, standing in a city full of your own people.

Facebook groups are the usual fallback, and they sort by topic and recency. A potluck eight miles away looks exactly like a potluck in another state. kAppit sorts by the thing that actually matters when you want to meet someone: how far away they are.

What's on the map

People

Kababayan nearby who have chosen to be visible. Kapit someone — that's the word for connecting here — and start talking.

Food share

Somebody cooked too much and has seats at the table tonight. It has its own place on the map because it is the easiest reason in the world to meet a stranger.

Events

Birthdays, karaoke, basketball, church, hikes, or just people getting together. Anyone can host one.

Filipino businesses

The restaurants, bakeries and supermarkets already feeding the neighbourhood. Owners can claim their listing.

Nobody gets your address

Putting people on a map is the kind of thing that can get somebody hurt, so this part was built before any of the rest of it.

Your pin is deliberately in the wrong placeEveryone and everything renders 200–400 metres from where it really is. The offset is fixed per person, so your pin never drifts and nobody can average it back to a real address.
Your real location never leaves the databaseThe app itself is never sent your exact coordinates. Not the map, not other people, not us.
Addresses are given, not postedA host's address only reaches you after that host confirms you're coming.
You can be invisibleBeing on the map is a choice you make and can take back. Dating is off unless you turn it on, and you only see other people who turned it on too.

Kumusta — I'm Kap

Short for kapitan, the barangay captain everyone back home knows by name. I'll show you around the first time and then get out of your way.

kAppit is built by Jerome Bustarga, who moved from Pili, Camarines Sur to Chula Vista as an international student and spent his first months there not knowing where anybody was. Kapit means to hold on — to a person, to a railing, to each other. It's what you say on a crowded jeepney, and the app is hiding inside the middle of it.

Sakay na, kapit na.

Get on, hold on. kAppit launches first in the South Bay of San Diego. If you want to know when it opens where you are, follow along — that's where the launch gets announced.
