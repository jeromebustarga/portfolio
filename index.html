Background

The short version: design came first, code came later, and the gap between them is where I like to work.

I have been fond of designing since I was fifteen. I would volunteer to make invitations, banners and flyers for family occasions, weddings, birthdays and my cousins' school projects. It was something I could give people before I had anything else to give.

There was a stretch when I was an out-of-school youth, and I really felt like I had fallen behind. I stepped back from my own education so my siblings could continue theirs. Around that time my mom and I started a bakery and pizzeria, and I handled everything on the front: the marketing, the banners and flyers and calling cards, the social media, even making the pizza boxes.

I finished high school as valedictorian and was accepted to five schools that offered me scholarships, though none were enough on their own. I shared my story online and my provincial government recognized it.

At Creighton University I started as a physics major on the engineering track. One of the requirements was a computer science class, and the moment I took my first one I fell in love, so I switched my major.

I know the reality of being in the margins, and I still consider myself part of it. I am not someone looking in from the outside. That is why my mission is to help the next generation find their voice and amplify it.
